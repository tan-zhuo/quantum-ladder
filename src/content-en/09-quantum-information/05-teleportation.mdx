Last section's conclusion felt a little frustrating: entanglement's correlations are strong enough
to violate the Bell inequality, yet cannot carry a single bit of messaging. This section shows what
entanglement is really for — not sending messages with it, but **sending states**.

## The problem at the door: how do you mail an unknown quantum state?

Alice holds a qubit in a state that **even she does not know**:

$$
\ket{\chi}=\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}
$$

She wants a faraway Bob to end up owning this state. Box up the particle and courier it?
Decoherence en route would destroy the state (chapter 10's subject). Then how about faxing:
measure it, phone the readings to Bob, and let him rebuild a copy?

## Two walls facing the classical approach

**Wall one: you cannot measure it all.** Section 9.1 told us a single measurement yields only 1 bit,
and the state collapses on the spot — the rest of the information in $\alpha,\beta$ goes up in smoke.
With a single specimen, $(\alpha,\beta)$ simply cannot be measured.

**Wall two: you cannot copy it.** Fine — photocopy a few first and measure at leisure? Quantum
mechanics welds that road shut too:

<Derivation title="The no-cloning theorem" level="基础" minutes={4} open={true}>

**Claim**: there is no unitary $U$ that achieves
$U\ket{\chi}\ket{e}=\ket{\chi}\ket{\chi}$ for an **arbitrary** unknown state $\ket{\chi}$
($\ket{e}$ being a fixed blank page).

**Proof (one clean blow from linearity).** Suppose it works on the two basis states:

$$
U\ket{0}\ket{e}=\ket{0}\ket{0},\qquad U\ket{1}\ket{e}=\ket{1}\ket{1}
$$

For the superposition $\ket{+}=\frac{\ket{0}+\ket{1}}{\sqrt2}$, linearity **forces**

$$
U\ket{+}\ket{e}=\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{11}}{\sqrt2}
$$

But "successful cloning" would require

$$
\ket{+}\ket{+}=\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{01}+\ket{10}+\ket{11}}{2}
$$

The two are not equal (section 9.2 computed the inner product: $1/\sqrt2$).
Contradiction — no cloning machine exists. $\blacksquare$

Linear evolution can only turn "copying basis states" into "creating entanglement" — CNOT's failed
photocopy was not poor craftsmanship but a law of nature. Incidentally: if cloning were possible,
section 9.4's entanglement correlations would become a faster-than-light telegraph (Bob could clone
a thousand copies and measure each to tell which axis Alice had measured along) —
so no-cloning and no-signalling are tied to the same rope.

</Derivation>

Cannot measure it all, cannot copy it — a dead end, it seems. The way out, found by Bennett and five
co-authors in 1993, is exquisite: **don't read it — let entanglement carry it for you.**

## The full protocol, by hand

Setup in advance: Alice and Bob each hold half of the Bell state
$\ket{\Phi^+}_{23}=\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{11}}{\sqrt2}$ (particle 2 to Alice, particle 3 to Bob).
Alice also holds particle 1, in the state $\ket{\chi}$ to be sent.

```text
particle 1 |χ⟩  ──●──[H]──[measure]──┐m₁ ─────────────┐
                  │                  │m₂ ──┐          │
particle 2 ───────⊕───────[measure]──┘     │(phone)   │(phone)
                                           ↓          ↓
particle 3 ───────────────────────────────[Xᵐ²]─────[Zᵐ¹]── |χ⟩
          Alice's side                 Bob's side
```

<Derivation title="Expanding the three-qubit state in the Bell basis: all four outcomes, none missed" level="进阶" minutes={9} open={true}>

**Step 0: the initial state.**

$$
\ket{\Psi_0}=\ket{\chi}_1\otimes\ket{\Phi^+}_{23}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(\alpha\ket{000}+\alpha\ket{011}+\beta\ket{100}+\beta\ket{111}\Big)
$$

(The three subscripts are particles 1, 2, 3 in order.)

**Step 1: put on different glasses for particles 1 and 2.** Rather than measuring the two particles
separately, Alice performs a **Bell-basis measurement** — asking "which of the four Bell states are
particles 1 and 2 in?" First invert the computational basis into the Bell basis:

$$
\ket{00}=\frac{\ket{\Phi^+}+\ket{\Phi^-}}{\sqrt2},\quad
\ket{11}=\frac{\ket{\Phi^+}-\ket{\Phi^-}}{\sqrt2},\quad
\ket{01}=\frac{\ket{\Psi^+}+\ket{\Psi^-}}{\sqrt2},\quad
\ket{10}=\frac{\ket{\Psi^+}-\ket{\Psi^-}}{\sqrt2}
$$

**Step 2: substitute and sort by Bell state.** Replace the particle-1,2 part of $\ket{\Psi_0}$ term
by term and patiently collect like terms (write out every one):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ket{\Psi_0}=\frac12\Big[
&\ket{\Phi^+}_{12}\otimes(\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1})_3\\
+&\ket{\Phi^-}_{12}\otimes(\alpha\ket{0}-\beta\ket{1})_3\\
+&\ket{\Psi^+}_{12}\otimes(\alpha\ket{1}+\beta\ket{0})_3\\
+&\ket{\Psi^-}_{12}\otimes(\alpha\ket{1}-\beta\ket{0})_3
\Big]
\end{aligned}
$$

**This is a pure identity** — nobody has done anything yet, and Bob's particle 3 already has four
states "pre-hung" on it, each just one Pauli gate away from $\ket{\chi}$.

**Step 3: Alice measures.** Each Bell state carries amplitude $\tfrac12$, so the four outcomes have
probability $\tfrac14$ each (**independent of $\alpha,\beta$** — Alice's readout contains no
information whatsoever about $\ket{\chi}$; we will need this later). After collapse, four cases:

| Alice obtains | Bob's particle 3 becomes | Bob's correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ket{\Phi^+}$ | $\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}$ | Do nothing ($I$) |
| $\ket{\Phi^-}$ | $\alpha\ket{0}-\beta\ket{1}$ | $Z$ (remove the relative minus sign) |
| $\ket{\Psi^+}$ | $\alpha\ket{1}+\beta\ket{0}$ | $X$ (swap back up and down) |
| $\ket{\Psi^-}$ | $\alpha\ket{1}-\beta\ket{0}$ | $ZX$ (first $X$, then $Z$) |

**Step 4: make the phone call.** Alice encodes her result into two classical bits $(m_1,m_2)$ and
sends them to Bob, who applies $Z^{m_1}X^{m_2}$ according to the table. Verify the trickiest row:
when she obtains $\ket{\Psi^-}$,

$$
ZX(\alpha\ket{1}-\beta\ket{0})=Z(\alpha\ket{0}-\beta\ket{1})=\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}=\ket{\chi}
$$

All four cases converge: **Bob's particle is exactly in $\ket{\chi}$**,
while Alice's particles 1 and 2 have collapsed onto a Bell state — $\ket{\chi}$ has vanished from
her side without a trace.

**Implementation detail**: the Bell measurement itself can be done with section 9.2's circuit —
CNOT (1 controlling 2) followed by $H$ on 1 maps the four Bell states onto the four computational
basis states $\ket{m_1 m_2}$; then just measure both (see the circuit diagram above).

</Derivation>

<div slot="left">

**What just happened?** No particle flew from Alice to Bob.
What was transported is the **state** — the entire structure described by the pair of complex
numbers $(\alpha,\beta)$ — which changed hosts through the "pre-laid pipeline" of entanglement.
The marvel: $(\alpha,\beta)$ are continuous parameters (writing them down takes infinitely many
decimal places), yet only a two-bit phone call was made. Where did the rest of the information come
from? It hid in the Bell pair — the Bell experiments told us entangled correlations outclass every
classical mechanism, and teleportation is the first time that super-strength was "cashed in" as a
working protocol.

</div>
<div slot="right">

The resource ledger (neither side of the equation can be shorted):

$$
1\ \text{ebit}+2\ \text{cbit}\ \longrightarrow\ 1\ \text{qubit}
$$

Consumable: the used Bell pair has collapsed; sending a second state takes a fresh pair.
The two classical bits cannot be skipped: the correction is a four-way choice,
and one bit short leaves Bob guessing.
Next section shows the "dual" of this ledger:
$1$ ebit $+$ $1$ qubit $\to 2$ cbit.

</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="Three misunderstandings that must be cleared up">
1. **Not faster than light.** Before the phone call arrives, Bob's particle is an equal-probability
   mixture of the four states — averaging over $m_1,m_2$ gives exactly $\rho_3=I/2$ (check it term
   by term), independent of $\ket{\chi}$. Bob can **measure nothing at all** before receiving the
   classical bits, and classical bits travel at most at light speed. The protocol's speed limit is
   the speed of the phone call.
2. **No conflict with no-cloning.** By the time the transfer completes, Alice's original has
   collapsed and been destroyed — there is only ever one copy of $\ket{\chi}$ in the world.
   This is "cut and paste", not "copy and paste".
3. **No matter is transmitted.** Bob's particle was in his hands all along; only its state changed.
   The science-fiction picture of "disassembling a person and reassembling them" goes wrong at the
   very first step — the disassembly.
</Callout>

<Callout type="insight" title="From thought experiment to engineering component">
In 1997 the Innsbruck and Rome groups gave the first photonic demonstrations; ions, atoms and
superconducting qubits followed; in 2017 the Micius satellite teleported photonic states from the
ground to an orbit 500 km up. But its true destiny is not as blunt as "long-distance haulage":
quantum repeaters use **entanglement swapping** (teleportation applied to entangled pairs
themselves) to cascade entanglement across continents; and in fault-tolerant quantum computers,
"gate teleportation" bakes hard operations into ancilla states in advance, then "pastes" them onto
the data with this section's protocol — an idea we will meet again in section 9.10.
Teleportation is quantum information's universal adapter.
</Callout>

## What comes next

Teleportation trades "1 ebit + 2 classical bits" for delivering 1 qubit.
What happens if you run the trade backwards — first hand Bob half an entangled pair,
then have Alice mail him **1 qubit**: how many classical bits does that buy?
The answer is 2: a single qubit can supposedly yield only 1 bit (section 9.1),
yet entanglement lets it carry an "overload". Next section: superdense coding.
