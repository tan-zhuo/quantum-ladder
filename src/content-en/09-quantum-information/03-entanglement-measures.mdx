Last section's CNOT built the Bell state $\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{11}}{\sqrt2}$,
and we proved it cannot be written as a product. But "cannot be written as a product" is only a
qualitative verdict. Twist the parameters a little:

$$
\ket{\psi_1}=\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{11}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\ket{\psi_2}=0.99\ket{00}+\sqrt{1-0.99^2}\,\ket{11}
$$

Neither can be written as a product; both are entangled. Yet $\ket{\psi_2}$ is almost $\ket{00}$ —
intuition says its entanglement is "much fainter". Intuition needs to become a number:
the Bell experiments of section 9.4 and the teleportation of section 9.5 both consume entanglement,
and **how much is consumed, and whether there is enough, must be computable**.

## First, lay the foundation: separable versus entangled

The general two-qubit pure state is

$$
\ket{\psi}=\alpha\ket{00}+\beta\ket{01}+\gamma\ket{10}+\delta\ket{11}
$$

If it can be written as $\ket{a}\otimes\ket{b}$ it is called **separable** (each qubit minding its
own business); otherwise it is **entangled**. The criterion fits on one line: a product state's
coefficient matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}$ has rank 1, i.e.

$$
\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{separable}
$$

A non-zero determinant means entanglement — and it even hints that the "amount of entanglement"
should have something to do with the size of $|\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma|$. We will come back to
that at the end.

## Where the old tools fall short: the wavefunction has no "local readout"

Chapter 3's state vector describes the whole system. But in an entangled state, **a single qubit
simply has no state vector of its own** — there is no $\ket{a}$ that correctly predicts every
experiment performed on particle A alone.
This is exactly why [section 3.10](/en/ch/03-formalism/10-density-operator/) introduced the density
operator: everything local lives in the **reduced density matrix**,

$$
\rho_A=\mathrm{Tr}_B\,\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
$$

(Tracing out B = averaging away all of B's possible outcomes, with their weights.) The key observation:

<Derivation title="The Bell state's reduced density matrix: globally pure, locally maximally mixed" level="基础" minutes={6} open={true}>

**Goal**: compute $\rho_A$ for $\ket{\Phi^+}=\frac{\ket{00}+\ket{11}}{\sqrt2}$.

**Step 1: write the full density operator.**

$$
\ket{\Phi^+}\bra{\Phi^+}
=\frac12\Big(\ket{00}\bra{00}+\ket{00}\bra{11}+\ket{11}\bra{00}+\ket{11}\bra{11}\Big)
$$

**Step 2: trace out B.** The rule: $\mathrm{Tr}_B$ keeps only terms whose B-side "left and right labels
match" — the partial trace of $\ket{i}_A\ket{j}_B\bra{k}_A\bra{l}_B$ is $\delta_{jl}\ket{i}_A\bra{k}_A$.
Term by term:

- $\ket{00}\bra{00}$: B side is $\ket{0}\bra{0}$, trace 1, leaving $\ket{0}\bra{0}_A$;
- $\ket{00}\bra{11}$: B side is $\ket{0}\bra{1}$, trace 0 — **the whole term vanishes**;
- $\ket{11}\bra{00}$: vanishes for the same reason;
- $\ket{11}\bra{11}$: leaves $\ket{1}\bra{1}_A$.

$$
\rho_A=\frac12\ket{0}\bra{0}+\frac12\ket{1}\bra{1}=\frac{I}{2}
$$

**Step 3: read off the physics.** $\rho_A=I/2$ is the **maximally mixed state** — the centre of the Bloch
sphere of section 9.1. Looking at particle A alone, every axis measures fifty-fifty; statistically
it is indistinguishable from "a coin that has been tossed but not yet looked at".
The cross terms (the coherences) were killed in the partial trace by the orthogonality on B's side:
**the global coherence has moved into the correlations, and locally only noise remains.**

Compare a separable state $\ket{a}\otimes\ket{b}$: the partial trace gives $\rho_A=\ket{a}\bra{a}$
directly, still pure.

</Derivation>

<Callout type="insight" title="Entanglement's essential signature: globally pure, locally mixed">
In the classical world, a definite whole implies definite parts: know the ordering of the entire
deck and you know every card. Quantum entanglement snaps this piece of common sense:
$\ket{\Phi^+}$ as a whole is a completely definite pure state (zero entropy),
yet each of its halves is a **maximally uncertain** mixed state.
The information is neither in A nor in B, but in "how A and B are correlated".
And so the strategy for quantifying entanglement follows naturally:
**the more mixed the parts, the deeper the entanglement.**
</Callout>

## Quantifying "mixedness": Schmidt decomposition and entanglement entropy

Any bipartite pure state can be brought, via the singular value decomposition, into **Schmidt form**:

$$
\ket{\psi}=\sum_k \sqrt{\lambda_k}\,\ket{u_k}_A\ket{v_k}_B,
\qquad \lambda_k\ge0,\ \sum_k\lambda_k=1
$$

where $\ket{u_k}$ and $\ket{v_k}$ are orthonormal bases on the A and B sides respectively.
For two qubits, $k$ runs to at most 2. The payoff is immediate:

$$
\rho_A=\sum_k\lambda_k\ket{u_k}\bra{u_k},\qquad
\rho_B=\sum_k\lambda_k\ket{v_k}\bra{v_k}
$$

**The two reduced density matrices have the same eigenvalues** — the Schmidt coefficients $\lambda_k$.
A single non-zero $\lambda$ ⇔ product state; two non-zero $\lambda$'s ⇔ entangled.
Weigh the mixedness with chapter 3's von Neumann entropy:

$$
E(\psi)\equiv S(\rho_A)=-\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_A\log_2\rho_A
=-\sum_k\lambda_k\log_2\lambda_k
$$

This is the **entanglement entropy** (base 2, measured in bits). Three benchmark points:

| State | Schmidt coefficients | $E$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product state $\ket{a}\otimes\ket{b}$ | $1,\ 0$ | $0$ |
| $\cos\chi\ket{00}+\sin\chi\ket{11}$ | $\cos^2\chi,\ \sin^2\chi$ | $0<E<1$ |
| Bell state | $\tfrac12,\ \tfrac12$ | $1$ (maximal) |

A state with entanglement entropy 1 is called **maximally entangled**; "spending 1 ebit" means using
up one such pair — the unit in which the protocols of sections 9.5 and 9.6 keep all their accounts.

<DualView>
<div slot="left">

**Why is entropy the "right" ruler?** Because it is not an arbitrary pick: one can prove that with
local operations plus phone calls (LOCC), the two parties **can neither create new entanglement nor
increase the entanglement entropy** — it is a resource that can be spent but never manufactured by local means.
And it is operational: from $n$ pairs with entanglement entropy $E$ one can (asymptotically) distil
about $nE$ Bell pairs, and conversely dilute $nE$ Bell pairs to prepare them.
"Entropy = the number of Bell pairs it exchanges for" — that is what earns it the name of a measure.

</div>
<div slot="right">

For $\ket{\psi_2}=0.99\ket{00}+0.141\ket{11}$:

$$
\lambda_1=0.9801,\quad\lambda_2=0.0199
$$

$$
E=-0.9801\log_2 0.9801-0.0199\log_2 0.0199\approx 0.141
$$

Under 15% of a Bell state — the intuition of "faint entanglement" now has a number.
Distilling a single Bell pair costs on average about 7 such pairs.

</div>
</DualView>

## The shortcut formula: concurrence

Entropy requires a partial trace and then a diagonalisation; for two qubits there is a shortcut.
The criterion determinant $\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma$ from the opening delivers the **concurrence**:

$$
C=2\,|\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma|,\qquad 0\le C\le 1
$$

$C=0$ is separable, $C=1$ maximally entangled. Its relation to the Schmidt coefficients is
$C=2\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_2}$, and it corresponds monotonically to the entropy:

$$
E=h\!\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-C^2}}{2}\right),\qquad
h(p)\equiv-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)
$$

Check it: $\ket{\Phi^+}$ has $\alpha=\delta=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$, $\beta=\gamma=0$,
so $C=2\cdot\tfrac12=1$; the state $\cos\chi\ket{00}+\sin\chi\ket{11}$ gives $C=\sin 2\chi$ —
as $\chi$ turns from 0 to 45°, the entanglement climbs smoothly from 0 to 1.

<Callout type="warning" title="Two common misunderstandings">
1. **"Entanglement = correlation"? More than that.** A pair of classical coins (tossed together,
   read together) is also perfectly correlated, but that corresponds to a separable mixed state.
   What makes entanglement special is being **correlated along every measurement axis**:
   a Bell state is perfectly correlated measured along $z$, and still perfectly correlated along $x$ —
   no separable state can manage both axes at once (separable states obey
   $|\langle ZZ\rangle|+|\langle XX\rangle|\le1$). Same-axis perfect correlations on their own can still
   be mimicked by a local hidden-variable model, though; the Bell inequality of the next section compares
   correlations between different axes, and that is what separates entanglement from every classical account.
2. **Mixed-state entanglement is not this simple.** This section's entropy and concurrence formulas
   hold only for **pure states**. Mixed states carry classical noise from the start, "mixed" no
   longer equals "entangled", and heavier machinery such as Wootters' formula is needed (not covered
   in this book). Deciding whether a general mixed state is entangled is, in general dimensions,
   even NP-hard.
</Callout>

To "see" these correlations, visit [Lab module 08: entanglement and Bell non-locality](/en/lab/08-entanglement/) —
the spectacle of two particles whose individual readouts are perfectly random yet strictly
correlated when combined is waiting there.

## What comes next

We can now weigh entanglement, but a more fundamental account is still owed.
Einstein's old objection is not answered by "the entropy equals 1":
perhaps the two particles secretly agreed on all their answers before separating — the so-called
correlations being nothing but two copies of the same crib sheet?
This "local hidden variable" hypothesis sounds unassailable,
yet it can be hauled before the tribunal of experiment. Next section: the Bell inequality,
and how quantum mechanics received its loophole-free final verdict in 2015.
