At the end of the last chapter we said: the entire edifice of quantum mechanics stands on the non-relativistic foundation $E=p^2/2m$. This section starts with a practical question — **when does that foundation give way?** The answer is much closer to home than most people expect: no collider required, the gold ring on your hand contains electrons moving at nearly the speed of light.

## Opening with a phenomenon: why is gold golden?

In the periodic table, silver is white, copper is red, gold is yellow. Silver and gold sit in the same group with the same electron configuration pattern ($d^{10}s^1$) — so why do their colours differ so much?

A metal's colour is set by "which light the electrons absorb". Gold's yellow comes from the $5d\to6s$ transition: that gap happens to fall at the blue-violet end of the visible spectrum (about 2.4 eV), so blue light is absorbed and the reflected light leans yellow. Silver's corresponding gap sits in the ultraviolet (about 3.7 eV), all visible light is reflected, and so it gleams white.

The trouble is: **a non-relativistic Schrödinger calculation puts gold's gap in the ultraviolet too** — computed gold comes out silvery white. Where is the discrepancy?

It lies in gold's nucleus of 79 protons. Estimate the speed of the innermost (1s) electron with the Bohr model (for a hydrogen-like 1s orbit around $Z$ protons, $v=Z\alpha c$, with $\alpha\approx1/137$ the fine-structure constant):

$$
\frac{v}{c}\approx Z\alpha=\frac{79}{137}\approx0.58
$$

**Sixty percent of the speed of light.** At that speed, relativistic effects are no longer corrections in the decimal places: the mass-increase factor is $\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-0.58^2}\approx1.22$, i.e. the "moving mass" is 22% heavier. A heavier electron means an orbital radius $\propto1/m$ that contracts, and the $s$ orbitals (largest probability near the nucleus, feeling the effect most strongly) contract hardest; $6s$ is pulled down, $5d$ is pushed up by the altered inner screening — the gap shrinks and drops from the ultraviolet right into the blue end of the visible. **The colour of gold is a relativistic effect visible to the naked eye.**

The same mechanism explains mercury: its $6s^2$ closed shell is relativistically contracted so low and tight that it barely shares electrons with its neighbours, the interatomic bonding is anomalously weak — and so mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature. Without relativity, mercury would be a solid at room temperature.

<Callout type="insight" title="A quick slide rule for relativistic chemistry">
To judge whether a system "needs relativity", one number suffices: **the inner electrons' $v/c\approx Z\alpha$**.

- Hydrogen ($Z=1$): $v/c\approx0.007$, corrections $\sim(v/c)^2\sim10^{-4}$ — exactly the scale of the fine structure in [section 6.6](/en/ch/06-hydrogen/06-fine-structure/): small, but visible to a spectrometer.
- Gold, mercury, lead ($Z\approx80$): $v/c\approx0.6$, corrections of **tens of percent** — the chemistry gets rewritten wholesale.
- Heavier superheavy elements: in predicting their chemistry, relativistic calculation is the only trustworthy starting point.

The fine structure was originally handled by "hand-stitching patches onto the Schrödinger equation" (relativistic kinetic-energy correction, spin-orbit coupling, the Darwin term). The patches work, but no one could say **where they came from**. This chapter goes looking for the single source.
</Callout>

## The formal level: how the Schrödinger equation offends relativity

Phenomena done, now the structure. The heart of special relativity is that **time and space stand on equal footing**: Lorentz transformations mix $t$ and $x$, so a covariant equation must treat them alike. Now lay out the Schrödinger equation:

$$
\ii\hbar\,\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\Psi+V\Psi
$$

<DualView>
<div slot="left">

**An asymmetry you can see at a glance.** The left side takes a **first** derivative in time, the right a **second** derivative in space. Boost to a uniformly moving frame and the Lorentz transformation mixes time derivatives into space derivatives — the first-versus-second structure falls apart at once. The equation looks different in different inertial frames, in direct violation of the principle of relativity.

Its "ancestry" tells the same story: the structure comes from translating the classical relation $E=\frac{p^2}{2m}$ into operators via $E\to\ii\hbar\partial_t$, $\vec p\to-\ii\hbar\nabla$. First power of $E$, second power of $p$ — the mismatch in derivative orders is rooted in this **non-relativistic** dispersion relation.

</div>
<div slot="right">

Relativity's energy-momentum relation is

$$
E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4
$$

Expanding in the low-speed limit ($p\ll mc$):

$$
E=mc^2\sqrt{1+\frac{p^2}{m^2c^2}}
=mc^2+\frac{p^2}{2m}-\frac{p^4}{8m^3c^2}+\cdots
$$

The $p^2/2m$ of the Schrödinger equation is only the second term: it discards the constant rest energy $mc^2$ (which contributes only an overall phase) and also drops $-p^4/8m^3c^2$ and everything beyond — and the first discarded piece is exactly the "relativistic kinetic-energy correction" patch of the fine structure in section 6.6.

</div>
</DualView>

<Derivation title="Estimate: when the corrections stop being negligible" level="基础" minutes={6}>

Take the ratio of the correction to the leading term as the criterion:

$$
\frac{p^4/8m^3c^2}{p^2/2m}=\frac{p^2}{4m^2c^2}=\frac14\Bigl(\frac vc\Bigr)^2
$$

**Inner electron of a hydrogen-like atom.** In the first Bohr orbit, Coulomb attraction supplies the centripetal force: $\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}=\frac{mv^2}{r}$, and combined with angular-momentum quantisation $mvr=\hbar$, dividing the two equations gives

$$
v=\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar}=Z\alpha c,\qquad
\alpha\equiv\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c}\approx\frac1{137}
$$

- Hydrogen: $(v/c)^2\approx5\times10^{-5}$ → corrections of order $10^{-5}$, which times the Rydberg energy 13.6 eV is $10^{-4}$ eV — precisely the measured size of the fine structure. **Consistent.**
- Gold: $(v/c)^2\approx0.33$ → the "correction" reaches 8%, with higher orders crowding right behind — patching order by order loses its meaning, and a relativistic equation is needed in one stroke.

**Another road to the same conclusion.** To localise an electron within a scale $\Delta x$, the uncertainty principle demands $\Delta p\gtrsim\hbar/\Delta x$, giving an energy fluctuation $\sim\Delta p\,c\gtrsim\hbar c/\Delta x$. Once $\Delta x$ shrinks to

$$
\lambda_C\equiv\frac{\hbar}{mc}\approx3.9\times10^{-13}\ \text{m (electron)}
$$

(called the **Compton wavelength**), the fluctuation energy reaches the order of $mc^2$ — enough to conjure particle-antiparticle pairs out of nothing. On scales below the Compton wavelength, the very concept of "the wavefunction of one particle" begins to fail.

</Derivation>

<Callout type="warning" title="The real abyss: particle number is not conserved">
Mismatched spacetime derivatives are only a "grammar error" — a different equation might fix them. The deeper difficulty is **physical**: relativity allows energy and mass to interconvert. Once the energy exceeds $2mc^2$, electron-positron pairs can be created; conversely, particles can annihilate into photons. Yet our entire formalism — one wavefunction $\Psi(x,t)$ normalised to 1, probability conservation (the continuity equation of [section 2.2](/en/ch/02-wavefunction/02-normalization-and-current/)) — assumes in its bones that **the particle number is always 1**.

So "relativistic quantum mechanics" is destined to be a transitional theory: the two equations of this chapter (Klein-Gordon and Dirac) will each expose their own cracks under the single-particle interpretation, and every crack points to the same exit — quantum field theory. Seeing exactly where the cracks are is this chapter's task.
</Callout>

## What comes next

Diagnosis complete — time to write the prescription. The most direct idea: since the disease is $E=p^2/2m$, swap in the genuine article $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$ and build a new equation by the same translation rules. That equation is the Klein-Gordon equation — it cures the covariance, and instantly detonates something stranger: **probabilities can go negative**.
