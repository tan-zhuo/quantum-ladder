Everything so far has dealt with **closed** systems: unitary evolution, pure states staying pure, no information lost.

Real systems are never closed. Air molecules hit them, thermal radiation shines on them, their own vibrations join in. This module asks what happens once the environment is included.

The answer is short — **coherences disappear extremely fast, and classical probabilities survive**.

<Scene name="decoherence" />

## From pure to mixed

A qubit's density matrix can be written

$$
\rho = \frac12\left(\hat I + \vec r\cdot\vec\sigma\right),\qquad
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^2) = \frac{1+|\vec r|^2}{2}.
$$

- $|\vec r| = 1$: pure, on the surface;
- $|\vec r| < 1$: mixed, inside;
- $|\vec r| = 0$: maximally mixed, at the centre, carrying no information at all.

**Decoherence is the process of $|\vec r|$ getting shorter.** The arrow contracting in the scene is exactly that.

## Two decays, two time scales

| Process | What it does | Time constant | Geometric picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase damping (dephasing) | Destroys $\rho_{01}$, the relative phase | $T_2$ | The ball is squashed into a vertical needle |
| Amplitude damping (relaxation) | System dumps energy and falls to the ground state | $T_1$ | The ball is dragged towards the north pole |

In general $T_2 \le 2T_1$: coherence goes at least as fast as energy. In quantum computing it is usually $T_2$ that limits you.

Switch the two channels on separately in the scene and watch whether the arrow is **squashed** or **dragged** — the two geometries are entirely different.

<Callout type="insight" title="Why macroscopic objects are not in superpositions">
The decoherence rate scales roughly with the number of environmental degrees of freedom coupled to the system. Rough magnitudes:

| System | Decoherence time |
| --- | --- |
| A single ion in vacuum | seconds to minutes |
| A superconducting qubit at 10 mK | 100 μs to ms |
| Large-molecule interferometry (C₆₀) | requires extreme vacuum to maintain |
| A dust grain (1 μm, room-temperature air) | $\sim 10^{-31}$ s |

The last line is the point: a dust grain's superposition decoheres in $10^{-31}$ seconds. It is not that superposition is forbidden, but that **it exists for far too short a time for any instrument to catch**.

So "the macroscopic world is classical" is not an extra rule bolted onto quantum mechanics. It is a conclusion quantum mechanics derives about itself.
</Callout>

## The Lindblad equation

Demand only that the evolution keep the density matrix positive and trace-preserving (a completely positive trace-preserving map), and assume the environment has no memory (the Markov approximation). The most general form is then

$$
\frac{\dd\rho}{\dd t} = -\frac{\ii}{\hbar}[\hat H,\rho]
+ \sum_k \gamma_k\left(\hat L_k\rho \hat L_k^\dagger - \tfrac12\{\hat L_k^\dagger \hat L_k,\rho\}\right).
$$

The first term is the familiar unitary evolution; the second (the dissipator) is the environment's contribution. Taking $\hat L = \hat\sigma_z$ gives pure dephasing; $\hat L = \hat\sigma_-$ gives amplitude damping. The two sliders in the scene are these two $\gamma_k$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Decoherence is not collapse">
Decoherence explains why we no longer see interference, and turns a superposition into something that **looks like** a classical probability mixture.

It does not explain why **one** outcome occurs each time. After the density matrix becomes $\mathrm{diag}(p_1,p_2)$, that object still describes an ensemble; getting from "a probability mixture" to "this run gave 1" is still the measurement problem.

The honest summary: decoherence solved the preferred-basis problem (why position, and not some superposition basis, gets singled out) and shrank the measurement problem considerably, but did not eliminate it.
</Callout>

## Think it through

1. Include the environment and the whole thing evolves unitarily as a pure state. Where, then, does the system's entropy come from? (Keywords: entanglement entropy, reduced density matrix.)
2. What does $T_2 \le 2T_1$ mean geometrically? (Hint: compare the shrinking rates of the ellipsoid along the equator and along the vertical.)
3. Error correction has to finish before decoherence destroys the information. If a single gate takes 20 ns and $T_2 = 100$ μs, roughly how many operations fit?
